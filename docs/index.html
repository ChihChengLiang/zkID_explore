Where could a privacy-preserving ID help in Taiwan?

A pipeline that looks for everyday situations where someone is asked for a whole document when the other side needed one fact, then screens the candidates for which are worth building.

What came out

246candidate use cases, from 33 scenarios
80pass all three tests: over-collected today, a narrow proof is enough, no legal duty blocks it
39sourced precedents used as prompts, quotes checked word for word
  • Strongest: member-only votes and tickets (unions, parties, building committees), lending across partner libraries, night-market stall permits, age checks at lockers and kiosks, protection-order proof, hospital status without the diagnosis.
  • Weakest: checks by banks, government counters, courts and telecoms, where the checker is entitled or required to hold the whole record.
  • Pattern: opportunities cluster where the body that signs is the body that asks, and no law makes it keep a copy.
  • Technology: in a 37-idea sample, about half need only an issuer-signed derived claim (no zero-knowledge proof); about a quarter need a credential nobody issues yet.
Treat as hypotheses. The candidates were generated and screened by AI models. A person checked only 30 pairs for duplicates. Legal verdicts are unverified (118 of 246 are marked unclear), nothing has been tested with a real verifier, and the certificate catalogue is partly recalled from memory.

The idea behind the tests

The screening borrows from Helen Nissenbaum’s theory of contextual integrity (Privacy as Contextual Integrity, 2004; Privacy in Context, 2010). Privacy, on this view, is not secrecy but whether information flows in ways that fit the norms of the setting it came from: a diagnosis passing from patient to doctor is fine, the same diagnosis passing to an employer is not.

A flow has five parts: who sends it, who receives it, who it is about, what kind of information it is, and the transmission principle, the condition under which it flows (consent, a legal requirement, a payment). It breaks the norms when any part departs from what the setting allows. That gives the three tests used here: does today’s request ask for more than the checker’s purpose needs, would a narrower proof still serve that purpose, and does a legal duty stand in the way? The theory does not say whether a better flow would actually be adopted, so that question is handled separately and is mostly unmeasured.
