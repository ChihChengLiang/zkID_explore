zkID Use Case Discovery — Taiwan MyData Worksheet

Purpose

Turn MyData's catalog of government-held personal data into a scored list of candidate zkID / selective-disclosure applications. MyData already shows where whole documents move between citizens, agencies and banks — each of those moments is a candidate for replacing a document hand-off with a minimal-disclosure proof.

How each row was built

  1. Category — one of MyData's 12 official document categories (找文件) or 9 service themes (找服務).
  2. Everyday moment — a concrete, recognizable situation a citizen or verifier faces.
  3. Predicate needed — the actual yes/no, threshold, membership, or match fact the verifier needs — this is what a ZK proof would output.
  4. Data disclosed today — what MyData / the paper document hand-off currently reveals to answer that predicate (usually far more than needed).
  5. Attribute source — the authoritative issuer who could sign the underlying claim.
  6. Frequency — how often an average citizen hits this moment (High / Medium / Low).
  7. Over-disclosure harm — what goes wrong today if the full document leaks or is retained (None / Low / Medium / High).
  8. zkID score (1-5) — see Scoring rubric tab.
  9. New attribute source unlock — attribute sources beyond classic government PKI that could open this use case to non-government issuers (banks, employers, telcos, platforms via zkTLS, etc.).

How to reuse this method outside MyData

  • Swap 'MyData category' for any country's equivalent registry/portal (e.g. Korea NPKI service list, EU Single Digital Gateway life events).
  • Any moment where 'predicate needed' is much smaller than 'data disclosed today' is a candidate.
  • Kill test: if law requires the verifier to retain the full document (e.g. AML/KYC record-keeping), a ZK proof alone does not remove that duty — flag as Partial, not High.
  • Discovery test: list who could sign the underlying fact even if they are not a government CA today (bank, employer, telco, platform) — that is where new use cases hide.

Scoring rubric

ScoreLabelCriteria
5Strong fitPredicate is a simple yes/no or threshold; trusted issuer already exists (gov CA, bank, employer); high frequency; verifier currently over-collects sensitive data; no legal requirement to retain the full document.
4Good fitSame as 5, but either frequency is medium, or predicate needs one extra field (e.g. selective disclosure of a category, not pure boolean).
3Possible / needs designPredicate is more complex (range, multi-source match) or the issuer ecosystem is fragmented (multiple CAs/counties); still worth prototyping.
2Weak fitVerifier has a legal duty to retain full data (KYC/AML) so ZK only reduces exposure in transit, not at rest; or frequency is low.
1Poor fitThe moment inherently requires full document content (e.g. a printed certificate to be physically inspected) or requires human judgment beyond a provable predicate.
